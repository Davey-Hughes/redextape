Getting around

  1. Type a program in the source view, or open one from examples ▾. It compiles as you type, and an error is marked where it is.
  2. Step it with the step controls: ▶ one step forward, ◀ one step back, ↺ back to step 0, play and pause at the chosen speed. A long run records as much as its budget allows, then offers to keep recording or to continue past its step cap. At the last step of a run that ends with a number, a Bool or a list, the TM, rxm and clif views mark the result with a tinted box and the word result: the first field of the REG tape, the register rr and the value main returns, which the rxm and clif views show whole in their first row. For a list, that is its pointer, and the cells the list reaches are tinted and edged too: the rxm and clif heaps light every one the frame holds, as one box where they sit together, and say how many more there are; the TM view boxes them on the HEAP tape where its window shows them. The λ view marks the term the same way: round it on one line, with the word after it when the line has room for it, or as one tinted block when it runs over several, the word after its first row, which has room for it wherever the view shows eleven columns. A run that ends with (), with a function, with a list of functions or with a value too large to print has no mark. Under the binary encoding the TM view's status line adds little-endian when it has room: a field's leftmost cell is its least significant digit.
  3. Click a token in the source, a λ token, an rxm or clif instruction or a machine rule, and the other views show the same point of the program.
  4. The view buttons in the header choose which views are shown: source, λ, TM, rxm and clif. The rxm view shows redextape's register machine, the IR between the source and the Turing machine: its control flow becomes the machine's states, and its registers, stack and heap become the machine's tapes. A software interpreter steps it, and it is not x86, ARM, WebAssembly or LLVM output. The clif view shows Cranelift's IR: what the native backends build from the rxm program, stepped one clif instruction at a time by the rules the compiled program runs by. Its as built and optimised buttons switch between redextape's lowering before Cranelift's passes and Cranelift's output at every opt level from O1 up, the same for every target. Its machine code panel shows the x86-64 or arm64 code Cranelift makes from it, as Linux calls it, in AT&T or Intel syntax on x86-64; each instruction that comes from an rxm instruction links it, and the panel marks and follows the code of the clif instruction about to run. Its step cap counts loop iterations, as the compiled program's does, not instructions. At the end of a run, the rxm and clif listings, the machine code panel and the state diagram's program level show the row the run stopped on. In the TM, rxm and clif views, a value, an instruction or a line cut short reads whole in a tooltip while the pointer rests on it. Each combination of views keeps its own arrangement, so views come back where they were left. A window about 640 px wide or narrower, at the browser's usual text size, has no room for the buttons; the workspace menu holds them there, in its show row.
  5. share makes a link to the program, the views and where each one stands; the workspace menu (Explorer ▾ at first) switches between the Explorer, Debugger and Stage presets; and settings holds the style, palette, appearance and keymap choices, and whether to format when leaving the editor.

To leave the editor by keyboard, press Escape and then Tab, or Ctrl+M (Shift+Option+M on a Mac) and then Tab. With the vim keymap, Tab and Shift+Tab move the focus in normal and visual mode and indent only in insert mode, so Escape and then Tab leaves from any mode; in normal mode >> indents and << takes an indent off. A line under the editor says what Tab does when you tab into it, and for as long as Ctrl+M has it moving the focus.

Every other key does what the editor's default keymap does, or vim's when settings sets the keymap to vim.

View keys

Press Ctrl+. and then one of these keys, from anywhere on the page, the source editor included. Ctrl may stay held for the second key or not. The key after the prefix acts once; any other key is typed as usual, and Esc, or Ctrl+. again, leaves without doing anything.

KeyDoes
]the next layout
[the previous layout
1show or hide source
2show or hide λ
3show or hide TM
4show or hide rxm
5show or hide clif

The layouts ] and [ step through are the source beside λ, the source beside TM, the source beside rxm, and all four.
