Getting around

  1. Type a program in the source view, or open one from examples ▾. It compiles as you type, and an error is marked where it is.
  2. Step it with the step controls: ▶ one step forward, ◀ one step back, ↺ back to the oldest kept step, play and pause at the chosen speed. A long run records as much as its budget allows, then offers to keep recording or to continue past its step cap.
  3. Click a token in the source, a λ token, an assembly instruction or a machine rule, and the other views show the same point of the program.
  4. A view's ⋯ menu has edit a copy: the term, the machine or the assembly as text you can change and run on its own. copies ▾ lists them.
  5. share makes a link to the program, the views and where each one stands; the workspace menu (Explorer ▾ at first) switches between the Explorer, Debugger and Stage presets; and settings holds the style, palette, appearance, keymap and format-on-blur choices.

Keys in the editors

F12go to the definition of the name at the caretthe source, and machine and assembly copies
Shift+F12go to the next reference to itthe source, and machine and assembly copies

A λ copy's editor has no definitions or references to go to, so these three do nothing there. Everything else is the editor's default keymap, or vim's when settings sets the keymap to vim.
